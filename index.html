FILE No. 006

Special Report: d4vd Apple Music Incident & Trial Update

Special Report
Summary An unauthorized track briefly appeared on singer d4vd's official Apple Music artist page during his ongoing criminal case, sparking viral speculation over whether the account was hacked or a distribution error was to blame; the song was later removed and no cause has been confirmed. Separately, a Los Angeles County judge has ordered d4vd (David Anthony Burke) to stand trial in the death of 14-year-old Celeste Rivas Hernandez. He has pleaded not guilty and remains presumed innocent, with his next court appearance set for August 31, 2026.
Full segment runs ~10 min — video not hosted here; thumbnail shown in place of playback.
Full segment script
Good evening. We interrupt our regularly scheduled programming for a special report involving singer d4vd and an unusual incident that has quickly spread across social media and music communities. A song unexpectedly appeared on d4vd's official Apple Music artist profile over the weekend, leading many listeners to believe that the singer had released new music. But there was one major problem. The song was not an official release. The track, reportedly appearing under the title "I DID IT…", was uploaded to d4vd's Apple Music profile during a highly publicized and sensitive period surrounding the criminal case against the singer. The appearance of the song immediately raised questions about how it got there. Was d4vd's account hacked? Was the song uploaded by someone who gained unauthorized access? Or could the incident have been caused by a problem involving a music distribution service? Those questions remain at the center of a growing online debate. [PAUSE] According to multiple reports, the song was not authorized by d4vd or his team and was eventually removed from Apple Music. The timing of the upload was particularly striking. The track appeared as the singer's preliminary hearing was taking place in Los Angeles, where prosecutors and investigators were presenting evidence in the case involving the death of 14-year-old Celeste Rivas Hernandez. Because of that timing, the unauthorized song immediately became a viral topic. Screenshots began circulating across social media, with some users initially believing that d4vd himself had released the track. Others quickly began questioning whether the artist's account had been compromised. The situation became even more controversial because online users connected the title and content of the song to the criminal case. However, it is important to emphasize that the track's appearance on the official artist page does not mean that d4vd personally uploaded or authorized it. Reports have described the upload as unauthorized, and the song was later removed. [PAUSE] The incident has also created confusion surrounding another major platform: YouTube. Across social media, users have been discussing reports of unusual or unauthorized music appearing on artist accounts and music pages. Some online discussions have claimed that d4vd's YouTube presence was also affected. However, as of this report, the evidence surrounding a direct hack of d4vd's primary YouTube account is not as clear as the reporting surrounding the Apple Music incident. What is clear is that unauthorized music appearing on official artist pages has become a topic of concern among music fans. And this has led to a much bigger question: How easy is it for someone to upload a song and have it appear under the name of a major artist? [PAUSE] Music distribution platforms allow artists and distributors to send songs to services such as Apple Music, Spotify, YouTube Music, and other streaming platforms. In some cases, music uploaded by a distributor can be mistakenly associated with an existing artist who has the same or a similar name. That means an incident that looks like a traditional "hack" may not always involve someone gaining direct access to an artist's personal password. It could potentially involve a distribution account, an artist profile, or a system that incorrectly connects an unauthorized release to the wrong artist. At this time, however, there is no publicly confirmed explanation for exactly how the d4vd upload occurred. [PAUSE] That uncertainty has not stopped speculation online. Some people believe someone intentionally targeted d4vd's music profile because of the ongoing criminal case. Others believe the incident may have been a prank or an attempt to create controversy. Another theory circulating online is that someone may have obtained access to previously unreleased material and then uploaded it under a provocative title. There has also been speculation about whether artificial intelligence or voice manipulation could have played a role in the audio. But these theories remain unconfirmed. There is currently no verified evidence establishing who uploaded the song, why it was uploaded, or exactly how it reached d4vd's official Apple Music profile. [PAUSE] The timing of the incident is what has made the story particularly unusual. The song appeared while the singer's legal case was receiving intense national and international attention. That meant that almost immediately after the song appeared, screenshots were shared thousands of times across social media. Some users treated the song as if it were a legitimate confession. Others questioned whether the entire incident was an elaborate hoax. Still others argued that the song may have been an old or unreleased recording that someone had obtained and uploaded without authorization. The result was a rapidly developing online story in which fact and speculation became difficult to separate. [PAUSE] For viewers following this story, it is important to remember that an artist profile on a streaming service is not necessarily proof that the artist personally uploaded every piece of content appearing on that page. Music can be distributed through third-party services and may be automatically delivered to streaming platforms. This is one reason why unauthorized or incorrectly attributed music can sometimes appear under the wrong artist profile. Whether that is what happened in this case remains unknown. [PAUSE] Meanwhile, the criminal case involving d4vd continues to move forward. A Los Angeles County Superior Court judge has ordered d4vd, whose legal name is David Anthony Burke, to stand trial in connection with the death of 14-year-old Celeste Rivas Hernandez. The decision came after a preliminary hearing in which prosecutors presented evidence and called witnesses. The judge determined that prosecutors had presented sufficient evidence for the case to proceed to trial. However, this decision does not represent a final determination of guilt. The purpose of a preliminary hearing is different from a trial. At trial, prosecutors will have to prove their case beyond a reasonable doubt. d4vd has pleaded not guilty to the charges and has maintained his innocence through his legal defense. [PAUSE] The singer is scheduled to return to court on August 31, 2026, for a post-preliminary arraignment. That date is not currently confirmed as the start of the trial. Reports indicate that the trial could potentially take place within the following weeks or months, but the exact trial date remains subject to the court's schedule and any legal proceedings that may occur before then. There have also been claims circulating online that the eventual trial will be held privately. At this time, we have not found reliable confirmation that the trial itself will be private. Therefore, viewers should be cautious about treating those claims as fact. [PAUSE] Regardless of the allegations and the evidence that has been presented in court, the legal principle of the presumption of innocence remains in place. d4vd is presumed innocent unless and until he is proven guilty in a court of law. The allegations against him remain allegations until a jury or court reaches a final verdict. [PAUSE] As for the mysterious Apple Music incident, many questions remain. Who uploaded the unauthorized song? How did it appear on d4vd's official artist profile? Was an account actually hacked? Was a music distributor involved? Was the material uploaded intentionally to create controversy? And what, if anything, does the incident have to do with the ongoing criminal case? At this time, there are more questions than answers. What we do know is that an unauthorized track appeared on d4vd's Apple Music profile during a highly sensitive moment, quickly went viral online, and was subsequently removed. The incident has now become another major point of discussion surrounding an already closely watched case. [PAUSE] For now, we remind viewers to be careful when sharing information from social media. Screenshots can be real while the claims surrounding them may not be. A song appearing on an artist's profile does not automatically prove that the artist uploaded it. And speculation about who may be responsible should not be treated as confirmed information until credible evidence becomes available. [PAUSE] We will continue monitoring this developing story, including any updates regarding the Apple Music incident, reports involving other music platforms, and the upcoming legal proceedings. The next major scheduled court appearance is August 31, 2026. Until then, questions surrounding the unauthorized music upload—and the circumstances that allowed it to appear on an official artist profile—remain unanswered. [SPECIAL REPORT MUSIC RISES] ANCHOR: Once again, d4vd has been ordered to stand trial in connection with the case involving the death of 14-year-old Celeste Rivas Hernandez. He has pleaded not guilty and is presumed innocent unless and until proven guilty in a court of law. His next scheduled court appearance is August 31. And as for the unauthorized music appearing online, the investigation and speculation surrounding the incident continue. We will bring you additional updates as more information becomes available.
